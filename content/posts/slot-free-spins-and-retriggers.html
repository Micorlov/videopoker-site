A free-spins round is a block of spins played without a fresh bet on each one. A retrigger is what happens when the round extends itself: the feature's trigger condition, usually a set number of scatter symbols, appears again during the bonus, and more spins are added to the ones still to play. What a retrigger does

Say a bonus starts with 10 free spins and the trigger is three scatters. On spin 4 three scatters land again, and a fixed top-up of 5 spins takes the counter from 6 remaining to 11. Each new spin can itself retrigger, so the round extends itself for as long as the scatters keep arriving. How the scatter requirement is set is covered in scatter symbol odds.

Three numbers describe the whole feature:

  • n, the free spins the round starts with.
  • p, the probability that any single free spin retriggers.
  • r, the number of spins a retrigger adds.

The maths: a geometric series

Treat the spins in generations. The first generation is the n you started with. Each spin retriggers with probability p and adds r spins, so on average every spin played creates p × r new spins. The first generation therefore creates n × p × r spins, those create n × (p × r)² more, and so on:

total = n + n(pr) + n(pr)² + n(pr)³ + …

When p × r is below 1 each term is smaller than the last, the series settles, and its sum is a standard result:

expected total spins = n ÷ (1 − p × r)

A quick check: if T is the expected total, the extras are p × r for every spin played, so T = n + p × r × T, which rearranges to the same formula.

A worked example with chosen numbers

These figures are picked to make the arithmetic easy. They are not the configuration of any particular game. Take n = 10, r = 5 and p = 0.04, one retrigger in 25 free spins. Then p × r = 0.04 × 5 = 0.2, and each generation is a fifth the size of the one before:

  1. Starting spins: 10. Running total 10.
  2. First top-ups: 10 × 0.2 = 2. Running total 12.
  3. Second: 2 × 0.2 = 0.4. Running total 12.4.
  4. Third: 0.4 × 0.2 = 0.08. Running total 12.48.
  5. Fourth: 0.08 × 0.2 = 0.016. Running total 12.496.
  6. Fifth: 0.0032. Running total 12.4992.

The totals close in on 12.5, which is what the formula gives directly: 10 ÷ (1 − 0.2) = 10 ÷ 0.8 = 12.5 spins. That is 2.5 spins more than the 10 on the label, an uplift of 25%. Notice that this is an average. With p = 0.04, the chance that a 10-spin round has no retrigger at all is 0.9610, about 66.5%, so roughly two rounds in three end at exactly 10 spins. The rest get at least one top-up, and the occasional long chain pulls the mean up to 12.5. RTP and volatility covers why an average and a typical round differ.

Why the curve turns upwards

The formula divides by 1 − p × r, so as p × r creeps towards 1 the divisor shrinks towards zero and the total grows without limit. Keeping n = 10:

  1. p × r = 0.1: 10 ÷ 0.9 = 11.1 spins, an 11% uplift.
  2. p × r = 0.2: 10 ÷ 0.8 = 12.5 spins, 25%.
  3. p × r = 0.5: 10 ÷ 0.5 = 20 spins, 100%.
  4. p × r = 0.8: 10 ÷ 0.2 = 50 spins, 400%.
  5. p × r = 0.9: 10 ÷ 0.1 = 100 spins, 900%.

Going from 0.1 to 0.2 adds 1.4 spins. Going from 0.8 to 0.9 adds 50. The vertical line at p × r = 1 is an asymptote: the curve never reaches it, and at 1 or above the series no longer settles, so the formula stops applying.

The average is also touchy: moving p × r from 0.8 to 0.85 takes the expected total from 50 to 66.7, a third more. Why designers keep retriggers rare

A designer who wants a bonus to feel generous but stay predictable keeps p × r well below 1. Near 1, a small slip in p moves the whole bonus a long way and the average is dominated by a few very long rounds. A low retrigger probability keeps the average steady while still allowing the moment of the counter jumping up.

That moment is worth more than the arithmetic suggests. A 25% uplift on average looks modest, but it arrives as an event, not as a smooth extra. A bonus with a generous retrigger feels dramatically better than its raw spin count because the count can keep rising, and the average only hints at that.

The uplift scales the whole bonus

The factor 1 ÷ (1 − p × r) multiplies the whole of the base round. If the ten free spins are worth some amount on average, the extended round is worth that amount times 1.25 at p × r = 0.2, times 2 at 0.5, and so on. It is a ratio, so it scales whatever each spin is worth, including spins that carry multipliers, as in multipliers on ways slots.

The short version: with n starting spins, retrigger chance p and r spins per retrigger, the expected total is n ÷ (1 − p × r) while p × r is below 1. For n = 10, r = 5 and p = 0.04 that is 12.5 spins, a 25% uplift, and the figure climbs steeply as p × r nears 1.
