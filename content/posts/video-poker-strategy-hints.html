Most hands have an obvious hold: keep the pair, keep the made flush, throw back the rubbish. A hint adds little there. It earns its place on the handful of hands where two holds sit within a few hundredths of a bet of each other and the one that looks wrong is right. Those hands are also where a hint copied from one game goes wrong in another, because the best hold depends on what the paytable pays.

All the figures below are exact averages over every possible draw, in bets, so a hold worth 0.483 returns 0.483 of a bet on average. The expected value post shows how one of these sums is built.

A hand where the hold looks wrong

You are dealt the ten of clubs, king of hearts, queen of diamonds, nine of clubs and six of hearts, on the full-pay 9/6 Jacks or Better table. Nothing is paired and nothing is suited. Four holds compete.

  • King and queen. Three cards are drawn from 47 unseen, so there are 16,215 draws. Of those, 5,022 make a pair of jacks or better (1 each), 711 make two pair (2 each), 281 make three of a kind (3 each), 84 make a straight (4 each), 18 make a full house (9 each) and 2 make four of a kind (25 each). The total is 5,022 + 1,422 + 843 + 336 + 162 + 50 = 7,835, and 7,835 ÷ 16,215 = 0.483.
  • Ten, king, queen, nine. This is four to an inside straight: only a jack completes it. One card is drawn from 47. Four jacks make the straight (4 each = 16). Three kings and three queens make a pair of high cards (1 each = 6). The other 37 cards pay nothing. The total is 22, and 22 ÷ 47 = 0.468.
  • Queen alone. 0.464.
  • King alone. 0.455.

The two-card hold wins by 0.015 of a bet. That is about one and a half bets lost per hundred such hands if you take the straight draw, which is small, and exactly why this is a hand people argue about.

The reason you can name

The inside straight looks stronger because it holds four cards, but it has four winning cards in 47, about 8.5%, and the prize is only 4. Its other income is the six high cards that pair a king or queen, which is the same pair of jacks or better the two-card hold is already aiming at. The king and queen hold has no single big outcome. It has many small ones: over 5,000 draws pair a high card, and about 1,100 reach two pair or better. In this price band, breadth beats a single narrow out.

That is a rule you can name, not a result to memorise: with only two high cards among the four, the straight draw has too few winning cards to be worth the cards you throw away. The Jacks or Better strategy post sets out the whole ordering of these holds.

The same hand on other paytables

The app lists Jacks or Better, Deuces Wild, Bonus Poker and Double Bonus. Deuces Wild is a different game, with its own table, so none of these figures carry over. The other two share the same cards. Taking the commonly published 8/5 Bonus Poker and 10/7 Double Bonus tables, the same hand returns:

  • Jacks or Better 9/6: K, Q 0.483; inside straight 0.468. Keep the two high cards.
  • Bonus Poker 8/5: K, Q 0.482; inside straight 0.468. The same call, with the gap barely changed.
  • Double Bonus 10/7: K, Q 0.449; inside straight 0.553. The straight draw now wins by 0.104.

Double Bonus pays 5 for a straight instead of 4, which lifts the four-card draw from 22 to 26 over 47. It also pays only 1 for two pair instead of 2, which takes 711 draws down from 2 to 1 and drags the two-card hold below 0.45. Two small changes to the paytable reverse the correct hold. A hint copied from Jacks or Better into Double Bonus makes this exact mistake, at a cost of 0.104 on each such hand.

A hold that does not flip still moves in size. Take 5♠ 5♦ K♦ 9♦ 3♦ : four to a flush beats the low pair on every table, but by 0.389 of a bet on 9/6 Jacks or Better (1.213 to 0.824), 0.207 on 8/5 Bonus Poker (1.021 to 0.814) and 0.661 on 10/7 Double Bonus (1.404 to 0.743). The call is the same and the margin is not, which is why the low pair against a four-card flush deserves its own post.

How to use a hint

  1. Check the paytable first. A suggested hold is right for one table. If the pay for a straight, flush, full house or two pair differs from the table you learned on, treat the hint as a question rather than an answer.
  2. Choose before you look. If you pick your hold, then see the hint, a disagreement is worth noticing. If you read it first, you learn nothing about your own judgement.
  3. Ask why on the close ones. A hint that differs from instinct by 0.015 is a small gap, and it is the explanation (breadth against a narrow out, a paytable that rewards straights) that carries over to the next hand. The cost of one wrong hold shows which errors are worth the effort.
  4. Relearn when you change game. The ordering of holds is a property of the paytable, not of video poker.
