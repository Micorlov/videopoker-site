In Fives and Threes a hand ends when someone plays their last tile, or when the line locks and nobody can move. Fives and Threes Dominoes lets you switch the going-out bonus on or off before a match, so it is a setting and not a fixed part of the game. This post is about the decision it creates: when to steer your last few tiles towards going out, and when a scoring play now is worth more.

What can and cannot be said about the bonus

The size of the bonus is the one missing number. The app listing says only that it can be switched on or off, and pub dominoes does not agree on an amount: some houses pay a share of the spots left in the other hands, others a flat sum. The post on how Fives and Threes scoring works sets no bonus figure, so this one does not invent one.

What can be done is to price everything else exactly and state the break-even. Call the bonus B. Chasing the finish pays only when B is larger than the points you give up to get there.

The scores the bonus competes with

Nine open-end totals score: 3 and 5 pay 1 each, 6 and 10 pay 2 each, 9 pays 3, 12 and 20 pay 4 each, 18 pays 6 and 15 pays 8. Everything else pays nothing.

The post on average score per hand turns this table into yardsticks. With a standard double-six set, 48 of the 98 possible pairs of ends score, and those 48 pay 133 points in all. A scoring placement therefore averages 133 ÷ 48, about 2.77 points. Seven tiles give a benchmark of about 9.5 points per hand.

That lets you read a bonus in units of plays. A bonus of 8 would equal one 15. A bonus of 5 would equal about two average scoring plays, since 2 × 2.77 is 5.54.

Holding a tile back

You can only go out if your last tile fits an end when its turn comes. A tile that matches just one number is a liability if that number disappears from the line. Holding back means keeping a flexible tile, one that shares numbers with others you hold, and playing a rigid one first.

The cost is a scoring play declined today. Suppose the ends allow a 4-point play, but it uses the tile that was your safe exit. If that tile is the only one you hold that can still reach an end, keep it. If you hold three tiles of one number, you have exits to spare and can take the points.

Tracking exits is the same work as counting tiles: two tiles that match a number still live in the line will go out in two moves, while two tiles of numbers already played out will not. When you cannot play, drawing from the boneyard adds tiles you then have to place.

The cost of the chase

Steering towards the finish usually means playing tiles that fit rather than tiles that score. Each skipped scoring play costs, on average, about 2.77 points. Two of them cost about 5.5 and three about 8.3.

That is the trade in a line. If B is below that, the chase loses on average, so take the points while the ends are good. If B is above it, finishing wins, but a longer chase gives the opponent time to go out first, and your starting hand size sets how long a chase can be. A failed chase is worse than none, because you gave up the scoring plays and did not finish; heavy tiles in hand are the other reason to play them early.

Rule of thumb: the bonus has to beat the points you give up. Two surrendered scoring plays cost about 5.5, three about 8.3, so a bonus below that is not worth a long detour.

When nobody goes out

A block happens when no tile left in the hands or the boneyard fits either end. Nobody plays a last tile, so nobody earns a going-out bonus. Many pub houses settle a blocked hand by comparing the spots left in each hand, but that is a house rule that varies, and the app listing does not say what it does here, so check the How to play screen rather than assuming.

The effect on your decision is the same either way. A block removes the bonus as a prize and leaves only the scoring plays you made on the way, so a hand heading for a block favours the player who has been scoring. The logic of closing a line is in blocking. If you lead and the bonus is on, steering into a block can beat a race the opponent might win.

To see the effect yourself, Fives and Threes Dominoes has the bonus switch in match setup, so you can play the same style with it on and off and compare your average score per hand.
