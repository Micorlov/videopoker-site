A hard 13, 14, 15 or 16 is the worst place to be in blackjack. It loses to any dealer total of 17 or more, and it busts on a good share of the cards that could improve it. Against a dealer showing 2 to 6, basic strategy tells you to do nothing: stand. That feels like giving up, but it is the stronger play, and the reason comes down to two fixed facts about the dealer and one about your own hand.

The rules assumed here are the ones the Blackjack Coach chart teaches: six decks, dealer stands on soft 17, double after split and late surrender. Hard means no ace counted as 11, so these are totals where the next card can only add to the count or bust you.

What standing is worth

The dealer draws to 16 and stands on every 17, so the dealer never finishes between 13 and 16. Your 13, 14, 15 or 16 therefore cannot win by comparison and cannot tie. It wins in one way only: the dealer busts. Every other outcome is a loss.

That makes standing easy to price. Your winning chance is the dealer's bust rate, and it is the same whether you hold 13 or 16. Using a six-deck shoe with the dealer standing on all 17s, the rates are:

  • Dealer 2: 35.4%
  • Dealer 3: 37.4%
  • Dealer 4: 39.6%
  • Dealer 5: 41.8%
  • Dealer 6: 42.3%

These are the figures in the dealer bust-rate post. Standing wins your bet about two hands in five against a 5 or 6, and a little over one in three against a 2. In terms of average result per unit bet, with an infinite deck for a clean comparison, that is a loss of 0.293 per unit against a 2, 0.252 against a 3, 0.211 against a 4, 0.167 against a 5 and 0.154 against a 6. Every one is a losing hand on average. The question is only whether hitting loses more.

What hitting costs

Each added point of total puts one more rank over the edge. Treating the 13 ranks as equally likely, with 10, J, Q and K counted separately, the share of cards that bust you on one hit is:

A hard 13 busts on 9 or any ten-value card, which is 5 ranks in 13, or 38.5%. A 14 adds the 8: 6 in 13, 46.2%. A 15 adds the 7: 7 in 13, 53.8%. A 16 adds the 6: 8 in 13, 61.5%. Bust and you lose at once, with no chance that the dealer fails afterwards.

Notice how it compares with standing. Against a dealer 6, standing wins 42.3% of the time, yet a 16 that hits loses 61.5% of the time before the dealer has even played. The cards that do not bust you rarely leave a hand that beats the dealer, either. They give totals such as 17 or 18, which still lose to a dealer who makes 19 or 20.

Where 12 sits

The same logic stops working at 12. It busts only 30.8% of the time when hit (4 ranks in 13), so hitting is cheaper than for any other stiff total. Basic strategy hits 12 against a 2 or 3, where the dealer's bust rate is lowest, and stands against a 4, 5 or 6. Against a 4 the difference is tiny, about 0.002 per unit bet in favour of standing, which is why it is the one cell people argue about. The full reasoning is in hard 12 against a 2 or 3.

Stand on 13 to 16 against a 2 to 6. Stand on 12 against a 4, 5 or 6, and hit it against a 2 or 3. Against a 7 or higher, all of these hands hit, with surrender an option for some of them.

Common mistakes

The usual error is standing on a stiff hand against a 7 or higher because hitting looks risky. The dealer's bust rate drops to 26.2% for a 7 and 21.2% for a ten, so waiting for the dealer to fail stops working. Hard 16 against a 10 covers that side of the chart.

The opposite error is hitting 13 against a 2 or 3 because the hand looks too weak to win. It is weak, but the dealer is weaker than the hand suggests, and an average loss of 0.293 beats the 0.308 you give up by hitting.
