Most blackjack cards have one value. Twos to tens count as their number, and jacks, queens and kings count as 10. The ace counts as 1 or 11, whichever suits the hand, and the choice is remade every time a card is dealt.

How a hand is counted

Add every card with each ace counted as 1. If the hand holds an ace and that total is 11 or less, add 10 more, which counts one ace as 11. That is the whole rule. A hand where an ace is currently counted as 11 is soft. A hand with no ace, or with every ace counted as 1, is hard.

Only one ace can ever count as 11. Two elevens make 22, which is already a bust, so a hand of ace and ace is soft 12 (11 + 1), not 22. Examples:

  • Ace and 6: 1 + 6 = 7, plus 10 is 17. Soft 17.
  • Ace, 6 and 4: 1 + 6 + 4 = 11, plus 10 is 21. Soft 21.
  • Ace, 6 and 5: 1 + 6 + 5 = 12, and adding 10 would make 22, so the ace stays at 1. Hard 12.
  • Ace and 10: 1 + 10 = 11, plus 10 is 21. Soft 21, and with two cards it is a natural.

The direction only runs one way. An ace counted as 1 never goes back to 11, because every later card adds to the total and a total that could not take the extra 10 before cannot take it now. A soft hand can become hard. A hard hand never becomes soft.

Why a soft hand cannot bust on one hit

The highest value any card can add is 10, and an ace drawn onto a soft hand counts as 1. Take the highest soft total, 21. Draw a ten and the total would be 31, so the ace drops from 11 to 1 and the hand becomes hard 21: 31 − 10 = 21. For a soft total t from 12 to 21 and a card worth c from 1 to 10:

  • If t + c is 21 or less, the hand stays soft at that total.
  • If it is more, the ace drops to 1 and the hand is hard at t + c − 10. The largest that can be is 21 + 10 − 10 = 21.

So no single card busts a soft hand. A hard 11 or less is also safe, since 11 + 10 = 21, but from hard 12 upward some cards bust it. Using the chance of each rank as 1 in 13, with tens, jacks, queens and kings together at 4 in 13:

  • Hard 12 busts on a 10, J, Q or K: 4/13 = 30.77%.
  • Hard 16 busts on a 6, 7, 8, 9 or any ten-value card: 8/13 = 61.54%.
  • Hard 17 busts on a 5 or higher: 9/13 = 69.23%.

Hard 17 and soft 17 are the same number and very different hands. One hit on soft 17 carries a 0% bust risk. One hit on hard 17 carries 69.23%.

How quickly a soft hand turns hard

A card keeps a soft total soft only if the total stays at 21 or under with the ace still at 11. That means the card's value, with an ace counted as 1, must be no more than 21 minus your total. At soft 17 that allows an ace, 2, 3 or 4: four ranks out of 13, so a 4/13 = 30.77% chance of staying soft. The other 69.23% of cards turn it hard, the same share that would bust a hard 17. The difference is that the soft hand survives as a hard total, anywhere from 12 to 17 (soft 17 plus a 5 is hard 12, plus a 10 is hard 17).

The pattern is regular. Each extra point on a soft total removes one rank from the cards that keep it soft, and each rank is 1/13, or 7.69%. At soft 20 only an ace keeps the hand soft, and it moves you to soft 21.

Soft means the ace is a spare. While it counts as 11 it can drop to 1, which is the hand's escape. Once it has dropped, the escape is spent.

The dealer follows the same counting

The dealer counts aces the same way, and Blackjack 21 Big Cards Offline describes its dealer as standing on 17. Whether a dealer stands or hits on soft 17 is a table rule in its own right: a dealer holding ace and 6 has soft 17, and where the rule is to stand, it stands even though a hit could not bust it. Three checks to run on any hand

  1. Is there an ace, and is it counted as 11? If so, the hand is soft and one card cannot bust it.
  2. If the hand is soft, which cards turn it hard? Count the ranks above 21 minus your total.
  3. If the hand is hard, how far is it from 21? From 12 upward, the ranks worth more than 21 minus your total are the ones that bust you.
