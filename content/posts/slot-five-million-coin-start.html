Why the length of a run matters

A slot spin has a large range of outcomes. In general, a game with 243 ways to win pays small amounts fairly often and large amounts rarely, and the rare ones do much of the work in the average. Slots are built this way as a rule; the post on RTP and volatility separates how much a game pays on average from how unevenly it pays.

The useful fact is how that unevenness behaves as spins accumulate. Add up n independent spins and the average result grows in proportion to n, while the spread around it grows only with the square root of n. So the spread per spin shrinks. Compare 50 spins with 5,000: that is a hundred times as many, but the total spread is only ten times larger, which leaves the per-spin spread at a tenth of what it was.

In practice, 5,000 spins sit close to the game’s long-run average, and a missing bonus or a lucky one is a small part of the picture. Over 50 spins the result is dominated by which rare event did or did not arrive. Two players with identical balances and identical bets can finish a 50-spin session far apart without either of them doing anything differently.

Bankroll in bets, not coins

This is the gambler’s-ruin idea in its simplest form. Whether a run of blank spins ends a session depends on how many bets you hold, not how many coins. A balance of 50 bets can be drained by an unlucky streak that a balance of 5,000 bets would hardly notice, and the chance of running out falls steadily as the bankroll-to-bet ratio rises. Ten-fold the bet and you have a tenth of the buffer against the same streak.
