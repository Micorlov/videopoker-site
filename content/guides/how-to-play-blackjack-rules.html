Blackjack, also called 21, is the most played table game in American casinos and one of the easiest card games to learn. You play against the dealer, not against the other players at the table. Your goal is a hand total closer to 21 than the dealer's, without going over. This guide walks through a whole round, then every decision you can make. Ace1 or 11, whichever is better for the hand.

Suits do not matter. A hand with an ace that can still count as 11 is called soft (ace + 6 is a soft 17). Once the ace has to count as 1 to keep you at 21 or under, the hand is hard. Soft and hard hands are explained in more detail here.

One round, step by step

  1. Bet. Each player places a bet inside the table minimum and maximum.
  2. The deal. Every player gets two cards face up. The dealer gets one card face up (the up-card) and one face down (the hole card).
  3. Blackjack check. An ace and any 10-value card as your first two cards is a blackjack, or a natural. It usually pays 3 to 2: a $10 bet wins $15. If the dealer shows an ace or a 10, they may peek for blackjack before anyone plays.
  4. Players act. Going around the table, each player plays their hand until they stand or bust.
  5. The dealer plays. The dealer turns over the hole card and draws by fixed rules.
  6. Payout. Beat the dealer and you win even money. Tie and it is a push: you keep your bet. Lose and the dealer takes it.

Your choices on each hand

Hit

Take another card. You can keep hitting until you are happy with your total or you go over 21, which is a bust: you lose straight away, even if the dealer busts later.

Stand

Take no more cards and keep the total you have.

Double down

Double your bet and take exactly one more card. It is the strongest move with a total of 10 or 11 against a weak dealer card, which is why players love doubling on 9, 10 and 11.

Split

If your first two cards are a pair, you can put out a second bet and play them as two separate hands. Always split aces and eights; never split tens or fives. Why aces and eights are always split.

Insurance

When the dealer shows an ace you may bet up to half your stake that the dealer has blackjack. It pays 2 to 1, but in the long run it loses money. Insurance explained, with the math.

Surrender

Some tables let you give up your hand after the deal and get half your bet back. It is the right play on a hard 16 against a dealer 9, 10 or ace. More on late surrender.

How the dealer plays

The dealer makes no decisions. In most American games the dealer must hit on 16 or less and stand on 17 or more. Some tables have the dealer hit a soft 17, which is slightly worse for you; the felt usually says which rule applies. What "dealer stands on soft 17" means. Because the dealer has to draw to 17, they bust often when their up-card is a 4, 5 or 6. Dealer bust rates by up-card.

Five rules of thumb for your first hands

  • Stand on 17 or more. Never hit a hard 17.
  • Hit 12 to 16 when the dealer shows 7 or higher; stand when the dealer shows 2 to 6 (hit 12 against a 2 or 3).
  • Double 11 against any dealer card except an ace, and 10 against 2 to 9.
  • Always split aces and eights.
  • Skip insurance.
