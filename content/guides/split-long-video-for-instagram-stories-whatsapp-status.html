Instagram Stories, WhatsApp Status, Facebook Stories and similar features all limit how long a single video clip can be. The limits change from time to time and can differ by app version and region, so check what your app currently allows. Whatever the number, the fix is the same: cut the long video into parts no longer than that limit and post them one after another.

Which part length to choose

Pick a part length at or below the destination's current limit.
DestinationTypical choice
Instagram Stories60 seconds, or 15 seconds if you want quick tap-through parts
WhatsApp Status30 or 60 seconds, depending on what your WhatsApp version allows
Facebook StoriesUp to the limit your app shows when you add a clip
Sending as chat messagesLonger parts are fine; split mainly to keep file sizes manageable

If you aren't sure, a slightly shorter part is the safe choice: a clip that is one second over the limit gets trimmed.

Step by step on Android

  1. Open a video splitter app and pick the long video.
  2. Choose the part length (for example 15, 30 or 60 seconds).
  3. Trim off anything you don't want at the start and end first, so the parts only contain what you will post.
  4. Preview the cuts. If the last part would be only a couple of seconds long, balance the parts so they are roughly equal.
  5. Save the parts. Good splitters number them (part 1, part 2...) so you can find them in your gallery.
  6. Open Instagram or WhatsApp, add the parts in number order, and post.

Tips for a clean result

  • Post in order. Gallery apps don't always sort by name or time, so go by the part number in the file name, or add a small "1/3" badge to each clip.
  • Avoid a tiny last clip. A 2-second final part looks like a mistake. Balanced parts look deliberate.
  • Cut on keyframes when you can. Cuts aligned to the video's keyframes can be copied without re-encoding, which is faster and keeps the original quality. The parts may then be a little shorter than the maximum.
  • Keep the original. Splitting creates new files; your original video stays untouched.
  • Mind private videos. Prefer an app that processes video on your phone rather than uploading it to a server.
