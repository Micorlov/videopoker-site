Jacks or Better is the base game of video poker: a pair of jacks or better pays, and the rest of the paytable climbs from two pair to the royal flush. Because you can see all five cards and the deck is a fresh 52 each hand, every possible hold has an exact expected value, and one of the 32 ways to hold a hand is always best. A strategy chart is that answer written as a priority list.

The paytable this chart assumes

The chart below is for 9/6 Jacks or Better, named after what a full house and a flush pay per coin. It returns about 99.54% with perfect play. If a machine pays 8/5 or 7/5 the game is worse, and a few close calls move, but the order below is still a good guide. See how to read a video poker paytable and what a full-pay machine is.

Worked examples

The chart earns its keep on the hands where two holds look reasonable. These are the ones players most often get wrong.

  • K♥ Q♥ J♥ 10♥ 3♥, a made flush. Row 2 beats row 3: throw the 3♥ and draw to the royal. You give up a sure 6 for a draw that averages more, because it can still finish as a royal, a straight flush, a flush, a straight or a high pair. More in breaking a flush or straight for four to a royal.
  • A♠ K♠ Q♠ J♠ J♦. A paying pair of jacks, but four to a royal sits higher. Hold the four spades.
  • J♣ J♦ 8♦ 5♦ 2♦. A high pair (row 6) beats four to a flush (row 8). Keep the jacks.
  • 6♣ 6♦ 9♦ Q♦ 2♦. Four to a flush (row 8) beats a low pair (row 9). Hold the four diamonds. The reasoning is in low pair or four to a flush.
  • 5♠ 5♥ 8♣ 7♦ 6♥. A low pair (row 9) beats four to an outside straight (row 10): keep the fives.
  • K♥ Q♥ J♥ 4♥ 9♠. Three to a royal (row 7) beats four to a flush (row 8). Hold K Q J of hearts.
  • A♦ K♣ 10♥ 4♠ 2♣. Two unsuited high cards are worth holding; a 10 of another suit is not. Hold A K.

Five rules that cover most of the chart

  1. Never hold a kicker. Keeping an ace next to a pair only lowers your chance of improving. See why a kicker never helps.
  2. Two pair: keep both. Never break two pair to keep the higher one (why).
  3. A low pair is worth keeping over almost every draw below four to a flush, even though it pays nothing on its own.
  4. Inside straights are not on the chart. With four to an inside straight and no high cards, the simple strategy throws it away. See inside vs outside straight draws.
  5. Play five coins when you play for real, because the royal flush bonus only pays in full on the fifth coin (the numbers).

How to practise the chart

Reading a chart and playing it at speed are different skills. Results in video poker are far too noisy to tell you whether your holds are right, so the useful practice is the kind that checks each decision. That is why free practice teaches more than playing for money.
