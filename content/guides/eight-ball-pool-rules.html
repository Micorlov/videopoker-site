8-ball is played with a white cue ball and fifteen numbered balls: seven solids (1 to 7), seven stripes (9 to 15) and the black 8 ball. One player takes solids, the other stripes. Pocket all of yours, then pocket the 8, and you win. Below are the common rules (close to the international standard); bars, leagues and apps sometimes change a detail or two.

The rack and the break

  • Rack the balls in a triangle with the 8 in the middle of the third row, a solid in one back corner and a stripe in the other.
  • The breaker shoots from behind the head string (the "kitchen").
  • A legal break pockets a ball or drives at least four balls to a cushion. If not, the opponent can usually re-rack or play on.
  • Pocketing the cue ball on the break is a foul. Pocketing the 8 on the break is usually not a loss: the breaker re-spots it or re-racks, depending on the rules.

Open table: choosing solids or stripes

After the break the table is open. Groups are decided by the first ball legally pocketed on a shot after the break: pocket a solid and you are solids. Until then you may hit any ball except that you can't use the 8 to make a group.

Your turn

  • You keep shooting as long as you legally pocket one of your balls.
  • On every shot the cue ball must hit one of your balls first, and then some ball must reach a cushion or a pocket.
  • In many rule sets you must call the ball and pocket for each shot; obvious shots don't need calling.

Fouls and ball in hand

Common fouls: pocketing the cue ball (a scratch), hitting the opponent's ball or the 8 first, hitting nothing at all, no ball reaching a cushion after contact, or knocking a ball off the table. After a foul the opponent usually gets ball in hand: they can place the cue ball anywhere on the table.

Winning (and losing) on the 8

  • Once all your group is down, you shoot the 8, calling the pocket.
  • You lose if you pocket the 8 early, pocket it in an uncalled pocket, or scratch while pocketing it.

How 9-ball is different

  • Only balls 1 to 9 are used, racked in a diamond with the 1 at the front and the 9 in the middle.
  • There are no groups. Every shot, the cue ball must hit the lowest-numbered ball on the table first.
  • Pocket the 9 on any legal shot, even early through a combination, and you win.
  • Fouls give ball in hand, and in many rule sets three fouls in a row lose the rack.

Beginner tips

  1. Hit the cue ball softer. Most missed shots are hit too hard. Medium pace keeps the cue ball under control.
  2. Aim at the contact point. Picture where the cue ball must touch the object ball to send it to the pocket: the point opposite the pocket on the object ball.
  3. Think one shot ahead. Choose the shot that leaves the cue ball near your next ball, not just the easiest pot.
  4. Clear trouble early. Break up clusters of your balls while you still have easy shots elsewhere.
