A dirt bike game is one of the best things to have on a phone with no connection: races are short, the controls are simple, and getting a lap right is satisfying on its own. But “offline” on a store listing can mean anything from “everything works” to “one practice track works”. This guide is a checklist for picking one, and a set of riding techniques that make you faster in almost any motocross or supercross game.

Test it before you need it

  1. Install the game and open it once while you are online, so any first-run download finishes.
  2. Turn on airplane mode and close the game fully.
  3. Open it again and play a career race, not just a practice lap. Finish it and check the result saves.
  4. Restart the game, still offline, and make sure your progress is there.

If all four steps work, the game will work on a plane, in the country or anywhere else without signal.

Riding tips that work in most motocross games

1. Win the start

The first corner decides more races than any other part of the track. Getting there first, the holeshot, puts you in clean air with no one to pass. Watch the gate lights rather than the riders around you, and go on green, not before: most games penalise a jump start or slow your launch. If your game has manual gears, don't hold first gear too long off the line.

2. Match the landing

The fastest jump is not the biggest one. Aim to land on the downslope of the landing ramp with the bike roughly parallel to it. Landing on the downslope keeps your speed; landing flat, or short on the face of a jump (casing it), scrubs it off and can throw you off. On a tabletop, coming up short is safe but slow. On a double there is a gap, so commit to the speed or roll it.

3. Use your body in the air

Most games let you lean the rider forward and back mid-air. Lean back if the front wheel is dropping too early, forward if you are about to land rear-first and loop out. Small corrections early beat a big correction just before landing.

4. Skim the whoops

Whoops are a row of tight bumps. Going slowly through them is the bumpiest and slowest way. Carry speed into the section and keep the throttle on, so the bike skims from crest to crest instead of dropping into every gap.

5. Brake before the corner, drive out of it

Do your braking in a straight line before the turn, then get back on the throttle at the apex. A berm, a banked turn, lets you carry far more speed than a flat corner; use the outside of it. Trying to brake while leaning is where most crashes in racing games come from.

6. Keep momentum in mud and water

Mud bogs and water splashes slow you on entry. Hit them straight and on the throttle; turning or braking inside them costs much more time.

7. Race your own ghost

If the game has time trials with a ghost of your best lap, use them. Racing yourself shows exactly where you lose time, and the improvement carries straight back into career races.

8. Tune last, and one thing at a time

Garage settings help once your riding is consistent. As a general rule, shorter gearing accelerates harder for tight tracks and taller gearing suits fast, open ones; softer suspension soaks up whoops and rough landings, stiffer suspension is more stable over big jumps. Change one setting, ride three laps, and keep it only if your times drop.
