Motocross (MX) is racing dirt bikes on long outdoor circuits built into natural terrain. Supercross (SX) is the same kind of bike on a short, man-made track inside a stadium. The bikes and protective gear are almost identical; almost everything else is different.

The differences at a glance

Typical real-world differences. Exact formats vary by series and country.
MotocrossSupercross
WhereOutdoor circuits in natural terrain: hills, sand, clayDirt hauled into a football or baseball stadium
Lap lengthLong, often well over 2 kmShort and tight, usually well under 1 km
JumpsBigger, faster jumps with long run-ups, plus hills and dropsMany tightly packed jumps: triples, rhythm sections, whoops
SurfaceGets rough: ruts, braking bumps and holes build up all raceSmoother and hard-packed, but tight with sharp turns
RacesLonger motos, often two per roundShort main events at night, under lights
What winsSpeed, fitness and line choice over rough groundPrecision, rhythm and a good start

How to ride a motocross track in a game

  1. Carry speed, not air. Outdoor tracks reward momentum. Pick lines that keep you on the throttle and use jump faces to keep moving rather than to fly high.
  2. Read the terrain. Uphills need a gear and throttle early; downhills need braking before the turn, not in it.
  3. Look further ahead. Long laps mean more surprises. Learn where the blind crests and off-camber corners are in practice laps.
  4. Set the bike up for rough ground. In games with a garage, softer suspension usually helps over braking bumps and whoops; taller gearing suits fast, open circuits.

How to ride a supercross track in a game

  1. Win the start. Laps are short and passing is hard, so the holeshot matters even more than outdoors. Launch on the green light, not before.
  2. Find the rhythm. Rhythm sections are sequences of jumps you can combine: double-double, triple-single and so on. Learn one combination and land each jump on the downslope to keep speed into the next.
  3. Skim the whoops. Hit a whoop section fast and stay on the throttle so the bike skims the tops; too slow and you bounce through every one.
  4. Brake early for tight turns. Turns come quickly after jumps. Square them off: brake in a straight line, turn, and accelerate out.

Common mistakes on both

  • Jumping as high as possible. Height costs time; landing on the downslope keeps it.
  • Braking while leaned into a turn, the most common cause of crashes in racing games.
  • Changing several setup options at once. Change one, ride three laps, keep it only if your time drops.
